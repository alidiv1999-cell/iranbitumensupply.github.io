Crude Oil Overview

Crude Oil is the primary petroleum feedstock used by refineries to produce fuels, petrochemical inputs, lubricants, asphalt, and many other downstream products. It remains one of the most actively traded global energy commodities for refiners, distributors, and industrial buyers.

What It Is

Crude oil is a naturally occurring hydrocarbon mixture extracted from reservoirs and traded in different grades depending on density, sulfur content, yield pattern, origin, and refinery suitability. Common commercial descriptions include light, medium, heavy, sweet, and sour crude categories. Buyers typically review assay data, API gravity, sulfur percentage, metals, water and sediment, pour point, cargo size, loading port, inspection terms, and delivery schedule before proceeding with a transaction.

Main Benefits

The main value of crude oil is its versatility as a source material for multiple high-demand petroleum products. Refineries can process suitable grades into gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, fuel oil, LPG, naphtha, base oils, bitumen, and petrochemical streams. Its global market structure, standardized inspection practices, and established tanker logistics make it practical for large-volume trade when commercial, technical, and compliance requirements are clearly defined.

Typical Applications

Crude oil is primarily used as refinery feedstock, but it also supports blending, petrochemical production, strategic storage, and energy trading portfolios. Demand is driven by transportation fuels, power generation, manufacturing, construction, and downstream chemical industries. International crude oil inquiries usually require detailed product specifications, proof of availability, loading and discharge terms, inspection arrangements, payment structure, and documentation standards.

Why Choose It

Crude oil is a practical commodity for qualified buyers that need large-scale feedstock supply and access to a broad range of downstream energy products. Selecting the right grade helps align refinery configuration, yield targets, logistics, and commercial objectives.
