What is Bitumen?

Bitumen—also known as asphalt in the United States—is a dense, black, highly viscous hydrocarbon material derived from crude oil refining or found in natural deposits.

It has been used for thousands of years as an adhesive and waterproofing material. In modern infrastructure, bitumen binds mineral aggregates in asphalt pavement, supports roofing membranes, and protects structures from water penetration.

Bitumen performance depends on viscosity, softening point, ductility, and its asphaltene/maltene balance. Choosing the right grade is critical for climate performance, load resistance, and long-term durability.
