Iran–Armenia Land Bridge: Bitumen into the Caucasus

Armenia is Iran's direct northern land gateway into the South Caucasus — a border relationship, similar in spirit to the Pakistan corridor, where bitumen can move overland rather than by sea. With regional interest in Caucasus transport connectivity continuing to build, Armenia's role as a transit and consumption market is one worth establishing early rather than waiting on.

Why the land route matters here

Iran and Armenia share a functioning border crossing that lets bitumen move directly north without transshipment, positioning Armenia as both a genuine domestic buyer and a potential bridge point toward Georgia and the wider Middle Corridor network linking Central Asia, the Caucasus, and Europe. For a market of Armenia's size, that overland option is a meaningful cost advantage over any competitor relying on Black Sea or Mediterranean shipping.

What Armenia buys

  • Bulk and drummed bitumen for domestic road construction and rehabilitation projects
  • Product positioned for potential onward movement into the broader Caucasus/Middle Corridor trade as regional transport links develop

Logistics reality

Shipments move overland from Iran directly across the shared border, avoiding the port congestion and multi-leg transshipment that affect sea-based competitors. We quote FOB ex-refinery/border for this route, since delivered pricing is primarily a function of trucking distance rather than vessel freight. We'll confirm current border and corridor conditions at the time of your inquiry, as regional transit arrangements in the Caucasus continue to evolve.
